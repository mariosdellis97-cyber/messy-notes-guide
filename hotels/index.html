01

What you're actually installing

Before any setup, worth being clear on what this is. Claude is an AI assistant you talk to in plain English, the same way you'd brief a new team member. It can read your email, check a calendar, draft a reply, and follow a set of instructions once instead of you repeating yourself every conversation.

A Skill is that set of instructions, packaged into one file. You install it once, and from then on Claude already knows exactly how your property wants requests logged, how bookings get confirmed, and how a review response should sound, without you explaining it again each time.

That's the idea behind this whole guide: teach Claude your system once, properly, and it runs it consistently after that.

04

The Front Desk OS

Four things run guest-facing work day to day: your inbox, your calendar, your guest requests, and your reviews. The Front Desk OS puts Claude in the middle of all four, reading and writing to each one instead of everything living in someone's head.

The rest of this guide is that setup, once, about twenty minutes total.

05 Setup, part 1 of 2

Connect your inbox

This uses Claude's built-in Connectors, a click-to-connect feature, not anything you need to install or configure by hand.

1
Open SettingsIn Claude, go to Settings, then look for "Connectors" (sometimes shown as "Search & Tools"). Exact wording shifts occasionally, look for the general idea.
2
Find GmailSearch the connector directory for Gmail.
3
Click ConnectSign in with the account the property actually uses for guest email, then approve the permissions it asks for.
4
DoneOnce connected, Claude can read and draft email in a normal conversation, nothing else needed on your end.

The Guest Log

One file, guestlog.md, that every workflow reads from and writes to. No new software, Claude creates it the first time it's needed.

Every request moves through four stages, with an urgent flag that jumps the queue:

New   Acknowledged   In Progress   Resolved   Urgent

Update it as the direct result of a real action, replying, resolving, confirming, never speculatively.

09 Workflow

A guest request comes in

Paste or forward the guest's message "Here's a guest request from room 214. Draft a reply that acknowledges specifically what they mentioned, not a generic apology. Then log it in the guest log."

Claude drafts, you approve, it sends and logs the request in one pass. Nothing goes out without you seeing it first.

10 Workflow

Confirming a booking

Paste with the dates + room type asked about "Someone's asking about a double room for the weekend of the 14th. Check the calendar for real availability, and if it's open, draft the confirmation with the actual dates, room, and rate."

Real availability, not a confirmation that turns out to be wrong once someone actually checks the calendar.

11 Workflow

The daily "who's still waiting" check

Ask once a shift "Who's still waiting on something?"

Urgent (1) — Theo Bryant, room 108, no hot water

Open 2+ hours (2) — Dana Mercer, one more

New, not yet acknowledged (1) — Nia Osei

Urgent always leads, regardless of how long anything else has been sitting.

12 Workflow

VIP and returning guests

Luxury hospitality runs on guests feeling remembered, not just accommodated. This workflow handles that, without ever faking familiarity that isn't real, a fabricated "we remembered you love the corner suite" lands far worse than a plain welcome when it's wrong.

Paste with the guest's name + arrival date "Jane Whitfield is checking in Thursday, she's stayed with us before. Check what we actually know about her and draft a short welcome-back note, nothing invented."
"So thrilled to have you back in your favorite corner suite!" — invents a preference that was never recorded
"Welcome back, we've held the quiet-floor room you asked for last time" — only what was actually noted
13 The compliance piece

Review responses, done safely

One more capability lives in the same system: responding to reviews, the ones that double as public marketing copy for every future guest reading them.

Every draft gets checked for accidental admissions of fault before it's delivered, especially on anything safety-related.

"Sorry our broken step caused your fall" — admits the step was broken and caused it
"Sorry to hear about your fall, we've had our team inspect the area" — acknowledges without conceding cause

Empathy and liability-safety aren't in tension. The good version still sounds human, it just doesn't concede anything that isn't actually confirmed.

14

If something doesn't work

"Claude says it can't access my calendar"

The Google connection may have expired. Go back to Settings → Connectors and reconnect, it's the same one-click flow as the original setup.

"The guest log seems to have disappeared"

Keep everything inside the same Claude Project. Files created in one conversation outside a Project may not carry over to the next one.

"Drafts don't sound like our property's voice"

Add a couple of lines about your brand's tone to the Project's instructions once, formal or warm, brief or detailed. It'll carry through every draft after that.

"Can more than one staff member use this?"

Yes, if everyone works inside the same shared Project, the guest log and the Skill are shared context for anyone with access.

15

The one rule that spans all of it

Draft, then confirm, then act. Claude reads freely, your inbox, your calendar, the guest log, no need to ask each time. But nothing gets sent, posted as a review reply, or marked resolved without you saying go, even if you told it once to "just handle guest replies from now on."

A slow reply is recoverable. A review response that admits fault in a safety complaint, or an email confirming the wrong room, isn't. That asymmetry is the whole reason this rule exists.
