The Messy Notes Playbook

The four-pass system for turning any meeting into a client-ready recap with Claude

Marios · Vantpoint

01

Every meeting leaves you with two things

What got decided, and what has to happen because of it. Most people lose track of the second one somewhere between hanging up and finally sitting down to write it up, usually an hour later, tired, guessing at half of it.

It's not really a note taking problem. It's a timing problem. By the time you get around to writing the recap, you've already forgotten the thing that mattered most.

02

Why one prompt isn't enough

Almost everyone who tries this makes the same mistake. They paste in their notes, ask for a clean summary, and get something that reads well and is occasionally, confidently, wrong. A "maybe Friday" becomes a hard deadline. A passing comment becomes a commitment. It reads clean, so you stop checking it, and that's exactly when it costs you.

The fix isn't a better single prompt. It's not trying to do extraction, fact checking and formatting in one pass, the same way you wouldn't write, edit and proofread a client email in one breath.

I run this as four short passes instead of one. I call it the Four-Pass Method, and the prompts for each pass are right after you unlock this guide below.

03

The Four-Pass Method

Four short prompts, run one after another in the same chat. Each one has a single job, which is the whole reason it works better than asking for everything at once.

Pass 1 Extract Pull the raw facts, no summarizing yet
Pass 2 Verify Grade its own confidence, line by line
Pass 3 Structure Build the four-section recap
Pass 4 Deliver Reshape it for wherever it's going

Each pass takes the previous one's output as its input. You're not retyping anything, you're just saying "next" and pasting the prompt.

04

Extract — Pass 1 of 4

Before anything gets summarized, pull the raw material out exactly as it appears. This is the pass everyone skips, and skipping it is where the invented deadlines come from.

Paste with your raw notes "Read these raw meeting notes. Before summarizing anything, pull out four lists exactly as they appear, without smoothing them into full sentences yet: every person mentioned and what role they seem to play, every date, number or amount, every commitment or promise anyone made, using their own words, and every place the conversation trails off or leaves something unresolved. Don't interpret yet, and if something is unclear, copy it exactly as said rather than cleaning it up. This is a raw pull, not a summary."

You'll get something messier than a summary and that's the point, nothing has been quietly smoothed over yet.

05

Verify — Pass 2 of 4

This is the pass that actually earns the guide its name. Feed the raw pull back in and make Claude grade its own confidence before anything gets formatted.

Paste after Pass 1's output "Go through every commitment and every date in the list above and mark each one Confirmed, Inferred, or Unclear. Confirmed means someone stated it directly with no ambiguity. Inferred means you're reasonably confident but it was implied rather than said outright, add a short note on what you inferred it from. Unclear means you genuinely can't tell and shouldn't guess. Be harder on yourself here than feels natural, if in doubt mark it Inferred or Unclear rather than Confirmed."

Three tags, that's all you need:

Confirmed   Inferred   Unclear

They carry through into the final recap in Pass 3, which is what makes it trustworthy enough to send without a second read.

06

Structure — Pass 3 of 4

Only now, with everything checked, do you ask for the actual recap.

Paste after Pass 2's output "Using the verified list above, write the recap in four sections: Summary, three to four sentences, Decisions Made, Action Items, each with an owner, a deadline if there is one, and its confidence tag in brackets, and Open Questions, anything still unresolved or marked Unclear. Keep the confidence tags visible in the Action Items section, don't quietly drop them, they're the whole point."

A recap that says "Friday (Inferred, said as 'maybe end of week')" is more useful than one that confidently says "Friday" and is wrong.

07

Deliver — Pass 4 of 4

The recap from Pass 3 is the source of truth. Where it actually needs to live changes by situation, so reshape it with one more short prompt instead of writing it out by hand each time.

Client email
Team chat update
CRM / Notion log
Manager ping
Pick one, paste after Pass 3 "Turn the recap above into a short follow-up email to the client. Keep it direct, no corporate padding. Lead with the summary in one line, list the action items plainly, sign off simply."

Swap "email to the client" for "Slack update under 80 words" or "one-line status for my manager" depending on where it needs to go.
08

Adjust it to the room

Add one of these as an extra line before Pass 3, depending on what kind of meeting it was.

Client call

Be strictest about anything involving price, scope or deadlines when you grade confidence. Clients remember exact numbers even when they were said casually.

Internal standup

Weight blockers over decisions. If someone mentions being stuck or waiting on something, treat it as an action item even without a stated deadline.

Sales call

Add a fifth section called Next Step in the Pipeline, and list any objections raised as their own line under Open Questions.

Vendor negotiation

Treat every number as Inferred unless it was repeated back and confirmed by both sides in the conversation itself.

Performance review

Keep the Summary section factual and neutral. This recap may get read by someone in HR later, not just the two of you.

09

Where this still goes wrong

The Four-Pass Method catches most of this on its own. Worth knowing what it's catching.

Invented deadlines
One-shot prompt: "Deadline: Friday" — notes actually said "maybe end of week"
Four-pass: "Deadline: end of week (Inferred, said as 'maybe end of week', confirm exact day)"
Two people share a first name
Assigns the action to whichever Sam it guessed first
Add to Pass 1: "if two people share a first name, note both and flag which one owns each item"
Mistranscribed numbers
A spoken price gets passed through exactly as transcribed, typo included
Add to Pass 2: "flag any number that sounds unusually round or unusually precise for spoken conversation"
A call too long for one pass
The back half of a long transcript gets thinner treatment than the front
Split into two halves with two minutes of overlap, run all four passes on each, then one short pass to merge and dedupe
10

What it actually looks like

Raw notes

"ok so jen said pricing by fri maybe, need to loop sam in, deck still not done, ask about the logo again, follow up thurs?"

After all four passes

Action items

Jen: send pricing Inferred
You: loop in Sam Confirmed
You: finish deck Confirmed

Open question

Logo status Unclear

The recap doesn't just look tidier. It surfaces the thing the raw notes buried, that "Friday" was never actually confirmed by anyone.

11

Make it stick

Retyping four prompts every time you start a chat is how people quietly stop doing this after a week. Don't.

Create one Claude Project called something like Meeting Recaps, and paste the four prompts in as project instructions or reference files. From then on, every new call only needs one thing pasted into a fresh chat inside that project, the raw notes. Claude already knows the Four-Pass Method because it lives in the project, not in your clipboard.

Ten minutes of setup, once. After that, every recap is a paste and a wait.
