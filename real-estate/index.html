01

Every deal starts the same way

A lead comes in through your inbox, or a form, or a referral. You reply, you check your calendar, you write it down somewhere, maybe a spreadsheet, maybe just memory. Then you do it again for the next one, and the one after that.

None of these tools talk to each other. Your inbox doesn't know your calendar. Your calendar doesn't know who's actually still interested. By the time you sit down to figure out who needs a follow-up, half the answer is already forgotten.

The Agent's Operating System

Four things run a real estate business day to day: your inbox, your calendar, your pipeline, and your listings. The Agent's Operating System puts Claude in the middle of all four, reading and writing to each one instead of you carrying information between them by hand.

Setting this up takes about twenty minutes, once. After that, every workflow in this guide just works.

The Tracker

One file, pipeline.md, that every workflow reads from and writes to. No new software, no import, Claude creates it the first time it's needed.

Every lead moves through five stages:

Update it as the direct result of a real action, replying, booking a showing, never speculatively.

07 Workflow

A new lead comes in

Paste or forward the lead's message "Here's a new lead. Draft a reply that answers what they actually asked, don't send a generic template. Ask one clear follow-up question, not five. Then log them in the pipeline tracker."

Claude drafts, you approve, it sends and logs the lead in one pass. Nothing goes out without you seeing it first.

08 Workflow

Scheduling a showing

Paste with the property + who's asking "Jane wants to see 14 Maple Heights this week. Check my calendar for real openings, propose 2-3 times, and once I confirm one, book it and draft the confirmation email."

Real availability, not "let me know what works," which is faster for both sides.

09 Workflow

The weekly pipeline review

Ask once a week "Who do I need to follow up with this week?"

Overdue (2) — David Okafor, Priya Nair

Due this week (3) — Jane Ruiz, two more

Gone quiet (1) — no contact in 16 days, no date set

The value is the triage, not a raw dump of every row in the table.

10 The fourth piece

Listing copy, done right

One more capability lives in the same system: writing the listing itself, once it's ready to go live.

MLS description
Zillow teaser
Social captions

Every draft gets checked against Fair Housing patterns before it's delivered, phrases that imply who a home is "for" (family status, religion, disability) get flagged and rewritten, not silently shipped.

"Perfect for a young family" — flags under familial status
"A fenced backyard ready for weekend barbecues" — describes the space, not who it's for
11

The one rule that spans all of it

Draft, then confirm, then act. Claude reads freely, your inbox, your calendar, the tracker, no need to ask each time. But nothing gets sent, scheduled, or marked closed without you saying go, even if you told it once to "just handle replies from now on."

A missed reply is recoverable. A wrong email to a client, or a showing booked at the wrong time, isn't. That asymmetry is the whole reason this rule exists.
